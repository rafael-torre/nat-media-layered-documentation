# Business Overview

A comprehensive view of who the client is, how they operate, and what their current reality looks like. This is the first document anyone on the team should read when joining an engagement.

## Company Identity and History

National Media Inc. (legal name: National Media Research, Planning & Placement, LLC) is a precision audience intelligence firm founded in 1985 and headquartered in Alexandria, Virginia. The company has evolved over four decades from a Republican-focused political advertising agency into a unified audience intelligence and activation platform, though its core identity and client base remain firmly rooted in Republican politics. With approximately 44 employees managing $3B+ in media spend across 10,000+ vendors, National Media operates a high-leverage, capital-light model built on proprietary data infrastructure and analytical methodology rather than traditional headcount.

The firm's culture is explicitly outcome-driven and competitive, with language centered on precision targeting, winning elections, and identifying the "critical margins that determine outcomes." National Media has earned 75 industry awards in recent cycles (45 Reed Awards, 27 Pollie Awards, 3 Campaign Tech awards), and the organization positions itself as innovative—the Chief Innovation Officer, Adam Wise, is publicly visible and active in shaping industry narrative. The company's pedigree includes seminal contributions to modern political advertising: they built CMAG (Campaign Media Analysis Group, later acquired by Kantar Media) in 1992, invented data-driven linear TV buying in 1996, and played key roles in three presidential victories (Bush 2000, 2004; Trump 2016). Most recently, they were cited as the core intelligence provider for the 2024 Pennsylvania Senate upset (McCormick vs. Casey).

## Business Model

National Media operates primarily as a managed services firm, generating revenue through media buying commissions and/or fees on the $3B+ in media the firm places on behalf of clients. Secondary revenue mechanisms include research and planning fees, and partnership revenue from platforms like Kinetiq KPI (launched April 2025). The firm positions its value proposition as "one system that sees everything, optimizes continuously, and wins consistently," competing against fragmented, multi-vendor approaches.

**Known unknowns requiring clarification:** The reported revenue figure of $8.6M (from third-party aggregators) appears to significantly understate total economics relative to $3B+ in managed media. This gap suggests either: (a) the $8.6M figure captures only a portion of revenue streams, (b) the commission rate is extremely thin (~0.3%), or (c) different accounting classifications apply to media billings vs. net revenue. The actual pricing model—whether percentage-of-spend, flat retainer + performance fees, or project-based—is not publicly disclosed and will require stakeholder clarification.

## Organizational Structure and Culture

National Media operates with a lean, highly specialized team structured around a small leadership core. The CEO is Don McDonough; the Chief Innovation Officer (Adam Wise) is the most publicly visible executive and drives the company's technology narrative. Managing Director of Digital (Kara Watt) and a small number of media buyers (e.g., Michael Eyerman) round out the visible leadership. The apparent HR and operations function is minimal (Director of HR, Accounting Manager, Business Office Manager), suggesting a focus on keeping administrative overhead low.

With only 44 employees managing $3B+ in spend, decision-making is likely centralized around the CEO and CIO, with a bias toward fast execution over committee-style process—typical for a firm with political origins where speed, confidentiality, and competitive advantage are paramount. The organization's culture is high-performance and results-oriented; performance is measured against outcomes (victories, margins, audience match rates) rather than activity or process metrics. **Known unknown:** The internal structure of technology and engineering teams is not publicly visible, and will be critical to understand for any software product engagement.

## Existing Systems and Technology Landscape

National Media has built or operates six proprietary technology platforms that form an end-to-end audience intelligence and media optimization system:

| System | Purpose | Key Capability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience Platform™ | Universal data foundation | 220M+ voter profiles, 1,300+ data points per profile; 7M HCP profiles; 6M business profiles; real-time enrichment |
| PowerPanel™ | Behavioral audience mapping | 45M households tracked across 6 media consumption behaviors; identifies "gap" audiences competitors miss |
| Tripwire™ Intelligence | Semantic contextual targeting | 250B+ pages/month analyzed; voter-informed contextual signals; 70K signals tracked monthly |
| Baker™ Planning Tool | Algorithmic media optimization | AI-calculated media mix; 40% greater efficiency claimed vs. traditional planning |
| FCC Intelligence™ | Real-time competitive monitoring | Tracks competitor spending across 210 DMAs; identifies underpriced inventory and daypart gaps |
| KPI™ System | Political ad competitive intelligence | Tracks $40B+ in annual political spending; launched April 2025 via partnership with Kinetiq |

National Media also maintains partnerships with key vendors including Kinetiq (real-time TV ad detection and creative capture), Amobee (contextual web analytics and audience segment creation), and Samba TV (first-party viewing data for cross-device identity resolution). The firm has deployed clean room technology for privacy-compliant data collaboration (launched 2022) and achieves an 86% match rate for CTV household targeting versus a 62% industry standard.

**Known unknowns critical for software engagement:** The underlying architecture of these six platforms (whether internally built, vendor-wrapped, or hybrid) is unknown. The data ingestion and ETL infrastructure, hosting/cloud environment, API availability, internal tooling stack (CRM, project management, data pipelines), and the degree to which platforms are licensed externally versus used exclusively internally all require stakeholder discovery. Understanding how internal teams actually use these tools day-to-day—and where operational friction or capability gaps exist—is essential for scoping any software product work.
