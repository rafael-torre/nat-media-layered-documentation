## Decision-Making Structure

Decision-making at National Media is inferred to be top-down and centralized around a small leadership core of CEO and CIO. The firm's political DNA — where speed, confidentiality, and competitive advantage are paramount — makes committee-style approval processes unlikely. With only 44 employees, there are few organizational layers between strategic intent and execution.

In practice: **Adam Wise** is the confirmed primary counterpart — all decisions on scope, direction, and delivery flow through him. **Don McDonough** holds budget and commercial authority and is not involved in day-to-day engagement decisions. Formal sign-off on spend or contract-level commitments likely requires McDonough's approval, but product and delivery decisions are Wise's call.

> **Known unknown:** Whether there are additional senior stakeholders on the technical or platform side (e.g., a VP of Data, Head of Research, or a Platform Lead overseeing the six proprietary systems) is not visible from public sources and should be established in discovery.

## Key Relationships and Dynamics

Two dynamics are worth surfacing for how the engagement is run. First, there is a clear division of authority: Adam Wise owns all product and delivery decisions as the confirmed day-to-day counterpart, while Don McDonough controls the budget and commercial gate. Wise is both the engagement champion and the operational decision-maker, but budget escalations need to go through McDonough — meaning Wise can drive the work but can't unilaterally approve spend.

Second, a potential organizational gap exists if Kara Watt has indeed departed: the digital practice may lack a named senior owner, which could slow alignment on digital-facing tools or create ambiguity about who speaks for that function. This is either a risk (slower decisions) or an opportunity (Dualboot can help fill the capability gap), depending on what discovery reveals.
