## Summary

National Media Inc. is a precision audience intelligence firm founded in 1985 and headquartered in Alexandria, Virginia. Originally built as a data-driven Republican political advertising agency, the firm has evolved over four decades into what it positions as a unified audience intelligence and activation platform — though its roots and core client base remain firmly in Republican politics. The company is small (approximately 44 employees, ~$8.6M reported revenue) relative to the dollar volume it manages ($3B+ in media across 10,000+ vendors), which signals a high-leverage, capital-light services model built on proprietary data and methodology rather than headcount.

The firm's core differentiator is a suite of six proprietary technology products — Audience Platform™, PowerPanel™, Tripwire™, Baker™, FCC Intelligence™, and KPI™ — that together form an end-to-end system for audience definition, media planning, and competitive intelligence. This positions National Media as something between a traditional media buying agency and a data/SaaS company: it earns via managed services and media commissions, but its competitive moat is increasingly technological. The firm has been extending this model beyond politics into healthcare/pharma and corporate public affairs, though the political vertical remains the dominant and most battle-tested use case.

National Media has a strong innovation identity — 75 industry awards in two cycles, a Chief Innovation Officer (Adam Wise) who is publicly active in shaping industry narrative — and is actively investing in partnerships (Kinetiq, Amobee) that extend its data and intelligence capabilities. For an engagement to build software for them, understanding the six-platform architecture and how their internal teams use these tools operationally will be critical.

## Key Findings

### Company Identity & History

- **Founded:** 1985 (some sources reference 1981 as a precursor; the formal company launch is consistently cited as 1985)
- **Legal name:** National Media Research, Planning & Placement, LLC (also referred to as National Media Inc. and National Media Insights)
- **Headquarters:** 817 Slaters Lane, Alexandria, Virginia 22314
- **Headcount:** ~44 employees (sources: RocketReach, ContactOut)
- **Reported revenue:** ~$8.6M (third-party aggregator; likely understates total economics given $3B+ media under management)
- **Founder history:** Founded by Robin Roberts; Republican advertising strategist Alex Castellanos joined as partner in 1989
- **Significant milestones:**
  - 1992: Built CMAG (Campaign Media Analysis Group), which became the industry standard for political ad tracking; later acquired by Kantar Media
  - 1996: Invented data-driven linear TV buying using Scarborough Surveys
  - 2000–2016: Key role in three presidential victories (Bush 2000, Bush 2004, Trump 2016)
  - 2010: First integration of Nielsen panel with voter data
  - 2020: Launched advanced CTV household-level targeting platform (86% match rate vs. 62% industry standard)
  - 2022: Launched clean room technology for privacy-first data collaboration
  - 2023: Launched Voter Informed Contextual targeting
  - 2024: Pennsylvania Senate upset (McCormick vs. Casey) cited as signature recent win
  - April 2025: Launched Kinetiq Political Insights (KPI) platform in partnership with Kinetiq
- **Self-description language:** "Precision audience intelligence firm." "We find the critical margins that determine outcomes." Strong emphasis on winning, margins, and competitive advantage over opponents.
- **Industry awards:** 75 in two cycles — 45 Reed Awards, 27 Pollie Awards, 3 Campaign Tech awards

### Business Model

- **Primary revenue mechanism:** Media buying commissions and/or fees on the $3B+ in media the firm places. The $8.6M revenue figure likely reflects agency fee income, not total media billings.
- **Secondary revenue mechanisms:** Research and planning fees; licensing or SaaS-style access to proprietary platforms (unconfirmed — needs validation with client); partnership revenue from platforms like Kinetiq KPI
- **Value proposition:** "One system that sees everything, optimizes continuously, and wins consistently" — positions against fragmented multi-vendor approaches
- **Service model:** Managed service. National Media appears to provide media planning, buying, and optimization as a service rather than licensing tools for self-serve use. However, the KPI platform launch suggests a move toward partner/platform distribution
- **Pricing model:** Not publicly disclosed. Typical for a firm of this type to operate on: (a) a percentage of media spend, (b) flat retainer + performance fees, or (c) project-based fees for non-political clients
- **Key financial metric to probe:** The gap between $8.6M reported revenue and $3B+ managed spend suggests either (a) the revenue figure is partial, (b) the commission rate is very thin (~0.3%), or (c) different accounting for media billings vs. net revenue. Needs clarification.

## Implications

**For the `business-overview.md` document:**
- Company Identity and History can be written with high confidence from public sources. The CMAG origin and the four-decade timeline of innovation are important for conveying the firm's credibility and DNA.
- Business Model section will need a caveat: the gap between reported revenue and managed media spend needs clarification. The final document should either resolve this or flag it as a known unknown.
- Customer Segments can be structured cleanly using the table above, with a note that healthcare and corporate are growth verticals relative to the core political identity.
- Organizational Structure will be thin without a stakeholder conversation. The document can state what is known (lean, ~44 people, named leadership) but should flag that internal structure — especially for technology — is unknown.
- Technology Landscape can be populated with the six-platform architecture. The key implication for a software engagement is whether we are building something that integrates with, extends, or replaces one or more of these systems — and that requires a stakeholder conversation.

**For the broader software product engagement:**
- The six proprietary platforms suggest National Media has non-trivial internal engineering capability or at minimum a strong product vision. Any software we build needs to fit coherently within or alongside this ecosystem.
- The heavy emphasis on competitive intelligence and real-time data throughout their platform language means any software we build should be designed for speed, reliability, and data freshness.
- The lean team size (~44 people) means we should expect limited internal IT support capacity. Deployment and operations burden will likely fall on us or require very low-touch infrastructure.
- The political cycle drives concentrated demand (even years are election years). If we are building operational tooling, there will be a hard deadline pressure that maps to election cycles.

## Gaps and Limitations

1. **Revenue model not confirmed.** The $8.6M revenue figure is from a third-party aggregator and likely doesn't capture total billings or media commissions. The actual commercial model, pricing structure, and margin profile are unknown.
2. **Internal org chart is incomplete.** We know the CEO and CIO by name; the rest of the team and any internal technology/engineering function are not visible from public sources.
3. **Technology stack depth is unknown.** We know the six product names and their capabilities at a marketing level. We do not know the underlying architecture, data infrastructure, or how teams actually use these tools day-to-day.
4. **Vendor and API relationships unclear.** Partnerships with Kinetiq and Amobee are known; others (data brokers, identity resolution providers) are likely but not confirmed.
5. **Healthcare and corporate verticals are not well-documented.** These are described as growth areas on the website, but no specific named clients, case studies, or revenue attribution are available publicly.
6. **No information on internal pain points or technology debt.** Without a discovery call, we don't know what problems are driving the software engagement — whether it's internal workflow friction, a gap in the platform stack, a client-facing product need, or something else entirely.
7. **Political affiliation creates a constraint context.** National Media is an explicitly partisan firm. This shapes client eligibility, partnership options, and likely the political environment any software product exists within. This isn't a limitation of the research; it's a known characteristic that should be surfaced in the final document.
