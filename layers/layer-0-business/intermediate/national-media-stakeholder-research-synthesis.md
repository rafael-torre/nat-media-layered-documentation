## Summary

Five individuals at National Media are publicly identifiable by name: Don McDonough (CEO), Adam Wise (CIO / SVP), Kara Watt (Managing Director, Digital — now departed per LinkedIn data), Michael Eyerman (Senior Media Buyer), and Jessica Beyrle (HR Director / Accounting Manager). Of these, Adam Wise is by far the most publicly active and the most likely day-to-day counterpart for a software build engagement, given his explicit ownership of the technology narrative and his direct involvement in the innovation and platform strategy that any software engagement would extend.

Don McDonough, as CEO, holds final authority on scope, budget, and strategic direction, but is likely to stay at arm's length from day-to-day delivery decisions unless something reaches a commercial or directional risk threshold. Michael Eyerman, as Senior Media Buyer, is the most likely operational end-user of any tool that touches media buying workflows. Kara Watt's departure (no longer at the company per LinkedIn as of 2021) means her role as Managing Director, Digital may be vacant, filled by someone else, or absorbed by Adam Wise — this needs validation.

The decision-making structure inferred from public materials is top-down and fast, consistent with a political media firm where speed and competitive advantage are core values. Formal committee-style approval processes are unlikely.

## Key Findings

### Don McDonough — Chief Executive Officer

- **Tenure:** Long-tenured; no public start date identified, but assumed to be a long-time leader given the firm's 40-year history and tight leadership structure
- **Public presence:** Minimal. Does not appear in industry press or public conference speaker lists. No LinkedIn profile identified in search results. His name appears in third-party aggregator data but with no public commentary or bylines
- **Role signal:** Classic CEO-of-a-small-firm profile: final authority on contracts, strategic direction, and major scope decisions, but not operationally involved in delivery. In a 44-person firm, the CEO likely also carries key client relationships directly
- **Likely concerns in a software engagement:** Commercial value delivered, fit with the firm's competitive positioning, speed to deployment, cost, and impact on client outcomes. Will want assurance that any software investment reinforces (not dilutes) the firm's innovation identity
- **Known unknowns:** No public information on background prior to National Media, management style, or how he engages with technology vendors. His absence from public industry events contrasts sharply with Adam Wise's visibility — this may reflect a deliberate division of roles (Wise as external face of technology, McDonough as commercial lead)

### Adam Wise — Chief Innovation Officer (also SVP, NM Insights)

- **Tenure:** At National Media for several years; CIO title since January 2024. Also holds the title SVP based on the November 2024 Campaigns & Elections article (the dual title may reflect internal vs. external framing, or a title change in progress)
- **Public presence:** Highly active. Speaker at Advertising Week NY 2024. Regular contributor to industry press (Campaigns & Elections, The Crosstab, Pro Politics podcast). Shapes the company's technology narrative publicly
- **Demonstrated expertise:** Deep expertise in media buying fragmentation, cross-screen audience matching, contextual targeting, and data-driven optimization. Comfortable with technical depth (e.g., explaining NLP/AI-based semantic modeling, match rate mechanics, waterfall supply dynamics). Uses data and numbers fluently in public communication
- **Communication style:** Direct, assertive, data-heavy, and opinionated. Described own approach as "be bold, the more you tell the more you sell." Willing to call out industry BS (e.g., on inflated CPP ratings, hidden fees). Has a competitive, outcome-focused lens on everything
- **Confirmed role in this engagement:** Primary day-to-day counterpart. All decisions on scope, direction, and delivery flow through him. He owns the technology strategy, has driven the six-platform architecture, and is the person who defines requirements and evaluates delivery. Budget and commercial sign-off sits with the CEO (McDonough), not Wise
- **Likely concerns in a software engagement:** Does the software reinforce or extend National Media's data/intelligence advantage? Does it integrate cleanly with the six existing platforms? Does it increase operational throughput without adding friction? Is it built for election-cycle speed demands? He will scrutinize technical claims and will not respond well to vague or oversold proposals
- **Key signal:** In the Crosstab interview he described building an in-house ACR panel and a semantic NLP model for swing audience identification. This signals that National Media already has significant internal technical capability or at minimum a high tolerance for building custom solutions rather than buying off-the-shelf

### Kara Watt — Managing Director, Digital (status: likely departed)

- **Status concern:** LinkedIn data suggests Kara Watt's tenure at National Media was January 2010 to July 2021. She may no longer be at the firm. RocketReach still lists her, but third-party aggregators can have stale data. This must be verified with the client before treating her as an active stakeholder
- **If still at National Media:** She would own the digital media buying practice and be a key stakeholder for any digital workflow tooling. Her long tenure (10+ years) would make her a deep institutional knowledge holder
- **If departed:** The Managing Director of Digital role may be vacant, absorbed by Adam Wise, or filled by an as-yet-unidentified person. This creates a gap in the stakeholder map that requires a discovery conversation to resolve

### Michael Eyerman — Senior Media Buyer

- **Tenure:** At National Media since May 2016; promoted to Senior Media Buyer in September 2021. Based in Washington, DC
- **Background:** Progressed from Media Specialist → Media Buyer → Senior Media Buyer. Prior experience includes internships at Americans for Tax Reform and the U.S. House of Representatives — a typical entry path for a political advertising career
- **Likely role in a software engagement:** Senior Media Buyer is the most likely persona for any operational tooling that touches media planning, buying, or reconciliation. He would be an end-user of Baker™ Planning Tool, FCC Intelligence™, and likely any new workflow software
- **Likely concerns:** Does the tool actually reduce time spent on manual tasks? Does it give buyers better data at the point of decision? Does it interfere with established workflows? Buyers in this environment are under significant pressure during election cycles — any tool adding friction rather than removing it will face resistance
- **Public presence:** Minimal; no public press appearances identified. His voice on a software engagement will likely be practical and workflow-centric rather than strategic

### Jessica Beyrle — HR Director / Accounting Manager / Business Office Manager

- **Role signals:** Multiple operational titles (HR, Accounting, Business Office Manager) in a 44-person firm is typical — one person wears multiple hats. This is a back-office operations role
- **Public presence:** No public profile or press appearances identified
- **Likely role in a software engagement:** Very limited. Could be relevant for vendor onboarding, contract administration, or procurement processes if the engagement requires it. Not a decision-maker on scope or direction
- **Likely concerns:** Compliance, vendor credentials, contracting logistics

## Implications

**For `stakeholder-map.md`:**

- The Stakeholder Register can be populated with confidence for Don McDonough, Adam Wise, and Michael Eyerman, with appropriate caveats about what is inferred vs. confirmed
- Kara Watt's current status must be flagged as unconfirmed — the document should note the data staleness risk and flag this for validation
- Jessica Beyrle can be included as a low-influence, informed stakeholder for operational/contracting purposes
- The Decision-Making Structure section can be written with confidence as top-down and CEO/CIO-centered, with the caveat that this is inferred from the firm's size, culture, and public signals
- The Key Relationships and Dynamics section should note: (a) the deliberate division between Wise as external technology voice and McDonough as commercial lead, (b) the risk that Kara Watt's departure leaves a digital ownership gap, and (c) the likely tension between operational buyers (Eyerman) who want tools that reduce friction and the innovation/CIO function (Wise) who may want to push the technology envelope

**For the broader software engagement:**

- Adam Wise is almost certainly the person who will define whether a proposed software initiative is worth pursuing. Getting alignment with him early — and speaking his language (data, precision, competitive advantage, speed) — will be critical
- The absence of public information on Don McDonough means the commercial champion and final approver is largely unknown. Understanding how McDonough makes investment decisions should be an early discovery goal
- The possible Kara Watt departure creates a risk: if the digital practice lacks senior ownership, it may slow decision-making on digital-facing tools. It could also create an opportunity to position Dualboot as a temporary capability gap filler
- Michael Eyerman represents the ground-truth test for any workflow tooling. If it doesn't work for the buyers in the room, it won't survive the first election cycle

## Gaps and Limitations

1. **Don McDonough has no public footprint.** His background, decision-making style, and how he engages with technology vendors are entirely unknown from public sources. He is the most important unknown stakeholder.
2. **Kara Watt's current status is unconfirmed.** Third-party aggregators list her, but LinkedIn data suggests she departed in 2021. Must be verified in the first stakeholder conversation.
3. **No technology or engineering team identified.** Despite National Media's six proprietary platforms, no engineers, data scientists, or product managers are publicly visible. The internal technical capability and headcount in this function is completely unknown — which is a critical gap for a software engagement.
4. **No secondary leadership identified.** Aside from the five named individuals, no VPs, directors, or senior ICs are publicly visible. The firm may have additional stakeholders (e.g., a VP of Data, Head of Research, or Platform Lead) who would be critical to a software delivery engagement.
5. **Adam Wise's dual title (CIO vs. SVP) is unexplained.** The discrepancy between the CIO title used in most sources and "SVP" used in the November 2024 C&E article may reflect a title change, a dual role across the NM parent and NM Insights subsidiary, or a reporting discrepancy. Needs clarification.
6. **No client-side contacts identified.** If the engagement involves tools that touch National Media's clients (political committees, campaigns), those stakeholders are entirely unmapped.
