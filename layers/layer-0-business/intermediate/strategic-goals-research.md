## Summary

National Media's strategic direction is shaped by three converging pressures: (1) the 2026 midterm cycle, which is shaping up to be the most expensive midterm on record at a projected $10.8B in political ad spend; (2) a structural shift in how audiences consume media — accelerating fragmentation that is making traditional linear TV buying insufficient and demanding more integrated, data-driven approaches; and (3) a deliberate push to diversify revenue beyond the political cycle into healthcare/pharma and corporate public affairs verticals that use the same underlying audience intelligence infrastructure.

The firm's stated strategic identity — "one intelligence system, total victory" — is not just marketing language. It reflects a real platform bet: that integrating data, planning, buying, and competitive intelligence into a single system is a durable competitive advantage against fragmented multi-vendor approaches. The April 2025 launch of Kinetiq KPI, and the homepage's teaser of a forthcoming "major partnership announcement" that promises "10x improvement in match rates" for down-ballot races, both confirm the firm is actively expanding its partnership-based platform surface area, not just its service delivery.

The constraints context is shaped by the political cycle creating hard, predictable deadline pressure (2026 election day), a lean 44-person team that limits internal capacity for absorbing operational change, and a security/confidentiality posture typical of political firms where client data is highly sensitive and speed of execution is paramount.

## Key Findings

### Finding 1: The 2026 Cycle Is the Primary Near-Term Strategic Forcing Function

- Total 2026 political ad spend is projected at **$10.8 billion** — the most expensive midterm on record, up ~20% from $8.9B in 2022. Early spending through August 2025 was already pacing ahead of prior cycles.
- CTV/streaming is the fastest-growing channel: projected at $2.48–$2.9B in 2026 (600% growth since 2020). This is directly in National Media's strategic wheelhouse — their CTV match rate (86% vs. 62% industry) is a named differentiator.
- Broadcast TV is declining as a share of spend but not disappearing; Adam Wise has been explicit that "TV still works and still moves the needle massively" and criticizes over-investment in news inventory and under-investment in premium programming.
- Down-ballot races (state legislature, school board, city council) now account for nearly half of total political ad spend — and are newly addressable via programmatic CTV. This is directly cited in the homepage's forthcoming partnership announcement ("revolutionary for down-ballot").
- **Implication:** For any software engagement, 2026 election day is effectively the hard deadline. Any new capability National Media puts in front of campaigns must be ready, stable, and trained on client staff before the heat of the cycle.

### Finding 2: A Platform-First Identity, Reinforced by Continued Investment

- National Media markets itself explicitly as a **platform company**, not just a services firm: "We're not just a media buying firm. We're not just data scientists. We're the unified audience intelligence and activation platform."
- The six proprietary systems (Audience Platform, PowerPanel, Tripwire, Baker, FCC Intelligence, KPI) are presented as an integrated, interdependent system — not a menu of standalone tools.
- The April 2025 launch of **Kinetiq KPI** marked National Media's first move toward an **externally distributed product** — a competitive intelligence platform sold to campaigns, agencies, journalists, and media organizations beyond their own managed services clients. This is a strategic shift: previously, the platform was internal competitive infrastructure. Now at least part of it is becoming a standalone product.
- The homepage teases another major partnership announcement described as "revolutionary for down-ballot" and a "10x improvement in match rates." The embargo had not yet lifted as of the homepage content (no specific date confirmed), but the framing suggests a new data or identity resolution partner.
- National Media won the **2025 Reed Award for Best New Firm or Product Rollout** — the specific product is not named publicly but likely refers to the KPI platform or a related new offering.
- **Implication:** The firm has an active product roadmap. Any software engagement should be understood in the context of an organization that already builds and ships products, has strong product instincts, and is likely to have opinions about architecture, integration points, and user experience.

### Finding 3: Revenue Diversification Is a Stated Goal, but Depth Is Unclear

- National Media explicitly presents three verticals with equal weight on the homepage: **Elections & Advocacy**, **Corporate Influence** (public affairs, B2B), and **Healthcare Outcomes** (HCP targeting, patient journey, clinical trial recruitment).
- The strategic logic is clear: the political cycle concentrates revenue in even years. Non-political verticals (healthcare, corporate) offer recurring, cycle-independent revenue using the same audience intelligence infrastructure.
- The healthcare capability is substantive in scale: 7M+ HCP profiles, 175M+ health signals, HIPAA-compliant workflows, condition journey mapping, clinical trial recruitment optimization. This is not a nascent offering — it has operational depth.
- The corporate vertical is less documented publicly. It focuses on C-suite/board-level influencer mapping, decision-maker journey orchestration, and cross-platform narrative amplification. Specific named clients are not public.
- **Implication:** If the software engagement touches operations, the system likely needs to support multiple vertical contexts with different data schemas, compliance requirements (HIPAA for healthcare), and client relationship structures — not just the political workflow.

### Finding 4: The Core Strategic Bet Is on Data Infrastructure as Competitive Moat

Adam Wise's public statements consistently return to the same theme: the media environment is fragmenting faster than campaigns can adapt, and the firms that win will be those with the best underlying data infrastructure — not just the best creative or the best relationships.

Key quotes that reveal strategic intent:
- *"You have to be smarter and more vigilant than ever today."* (on declining broadcast reach)
- *"Zero-party data… has never been more key."* (on data that customers voluntarily provide, enabling deterministic rather than probabilistic targeting)
- *"The buying and delivery of a message is almost as important as the message that's being delivered."* (2024 post-cycle takeaway)
- The Crosstab interview reveals the Tripwire/Voter Informed Contextual product in detail: 200B website visits processed monthly, 100M+ websites, 70K data points per device — explicitly built to solve voter file match rate problems at DSP scale.

This reveals a **data infrastructure expansion loop** as a core strategic pattern: identify a gap in how audiences can be found or reached → build or partner to create a new data layer → wrap it in tooling → differentiate against competitors who still rely on lower-fidelity data.

- **Implication:** Any software being built is likely contributing to or sitting on top of this data infrastructure. Speed, reliability, and data freshness will be non-negotiable product requirements — not nice-to-haves.

## Implications

**For `strategic-goals-and-constraints.md`:**

- **Strategic Goals** should center on: (1) winning the 2026 cycle at scale across political and down-ballot races; (2) expanding the KPI/competitive intelligence platform as a standalone product distribution channel; (3) advancing non-political vertical revenue (healthcare + corporate) to reduce cycle dependency; (4) continuing to extend the data infrastructure moat — match rates, signal volume, device coverage — as the foundation for all verticals.
- **Engagement Drivers** should surface: the 2026 cycle creates urgency; the KPI launch and forthcoming partnership signals active product expansion; and the gap between their 44-person team and $3B managed spend means operational efficiency is always an underlying pressure.
- **Constraints** will need a strong "Known Unknowns" caveat for budget and commercial terms, internal org structure for technology/engineering, and specifics of FEC/regulatory compliance. The HIPAA and election-cycle constraints can be stated with higher confidence.

**For the broader engagement:**
- The firm has product DNA and a roadmap. Come in with a systems perspective, not just an execution perspective. They will likely be opinionated about architecture and integration.
- Speed and data freshness are existential product values — not features. Any performance discussion will be framed in terms of beating competitors in real time.
- The non-political verticals are real and growing — any system that assumes a pure political context may need to be revisited as scope expands.

## Gaps and Limitations

1. **No stakeholder interviews.** All findings are from public sources. Strategic goals, engagement drivers, constraints, and budget are either inferred or flagged as unknown.
2. **The forthcoming partnership announcement is embargoed.** The homepage explicitly teases a "major partnership announcement" that was unreleased at time of research. This could materially affect the strategic goals or technical constraints document once announced.
3. **NMI's internal engineering team is invisible.** The degree to which their 6-platform suite is built in-house vs. vendor-wrapped is unknown. This affects the realistic scope of any software integration.
4. **Non-political vertical depth is soft.** Healthcare and corporate are presented at a marketing level. No named clients, revenue attribution, or operational workflow detail is public. Their actual maturity vs. the political vertical is unknown.
5. **Competitive landscape pressures are partially inferred.** Companies like AdImpact, L2 Political, Aristotle, and WinRed are operating in adjacent spaces. A more targeted competitive constraint analysis would be valuable once the engagement scope is confirmed.
6. **No confirmed revenue model or budget envelope.** The actual commercial structure of the engagement is not visible from public sources.
